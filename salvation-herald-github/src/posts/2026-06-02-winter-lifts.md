For seven days the temperature in Salvation had not risen above freezing. Then, in the span of a single hour on the afternoon of May 26, it climbed seven degrees. Ice sheeted off the eaves on Church Street and fell in the yards. The lake, silent since the first week of winter, was heard again from the Landing.

By the first of June the town had settled into the weather of an ordinary late spring in the Appalachians — warm mornings, pollen on the sills, the roads soft with runoff. But Salvation is not the town it was in April.

## A Town Twice Its Size

Its population has nearly doubled. Hackett's Camp, for years a seasonal encampment at the old fairground, has hardened over the winter into a permanent quarter that residents have taken to calling the Side. Cookfires burn there after dark now, and children play in the churned mud between the tents.

> "Some came back to us. Some didn't. And a good many we've never seen before are here to stay. You learn to set another place at the table."

Along the north road, families waited through the night of the thaw. Some greeted people they had buried in their minds months ago. Others waited and were not answered. County officials declined to give a figure for those still unaccounted for, saying only that the count is "ongoing and complicated."

## An Unwritten Peace

What remains is a fragile arrangement between the town's older residents and the newcomers — a peace, the Herald observes, that no ordinance has yet been written to hold. The selectmen meet Monday. Whether they can put the summer's hardest questions on paper is, for now, anyone's guess.
